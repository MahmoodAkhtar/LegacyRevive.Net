# LegacyRevive.NET — Terminology

## Status

**Canonical**

This document is the canonical glossary for LegacyRevive.NET terminology that is already sufficiently established.

The formal Evidence Model is defined in `03-evidence-model.md`.

This glossary reflects those canonical semantics while remaining the authoritative home for term definitions.

The qualification history showing how the Evidence Model terminology was settled is preserved in `91-design-qualification-register.md`.

---

# 1. Terminology principles

LegacyRevive.NET must use terminology in a way that preserves the distinction between:

- surviving/original material;
- direct observation;
- derived information;
- inference;
- assumptions;
- reconstruction;
- developer intervention;
- validation.

A term used in this glossary does not automatically imply that its complete lifecycle, data model, confidence semantics, or transition rules have already been defined.

Where a term is affected by an open qualification, that qualification is identified explicitly.

---

# 2. Artifact

An **Artifact** is a surviving file, package, resource, metadata structure, configuration item, executable output, or other technical item available to the recovery process.

Examples may include assemblies, executables, configuration files, PDBs, XML documentation, runtime metadata, package metadata, and resources.

An artifact may be original surviving material or may be produced later by the recovery process.

Use more specific terms where that distinction matters.

---

# 3. Original Artifact

An **Original Artifact** is an artifact that survived from the system being recovered and was not generated by LegacyRevive.NET or by a later recovery intervention.

Original artifacts form part of the primary historical evidence base.

They must remain distinguishable from reconstructed or generated artifacts.

---

# 4. Recovery Input

**Recovery Input** is any information deliberately admitted to the recovery process for the purpose of producing recovery conclusions or reconstruction state.

It may include:

- Original-System Evidence;
- Independent Contextual Evidence explicitly admitted to the recovery;
- Recovery Configuration;
- Developer Input.

Recovery Input is broader than Original-System Evidence.

Formal relationship: `Recovery Input` is broader than `Evidence`. Evidence and Developer Input are separate top-level categories beneath Recovery Input, while Validation Reference remains outside Recovery Input. `Supplied Recovery Input` is the subset supplied before or directly to the recovery; explicitly enabled External Enrichment may later be admitted as Independent Contextual Evidence without becoming Supplied Recovery Input.

---

# 5. Validation Reference

A **Validation Reference** is information used to evaluate a recovery result but deliberately withheld from the recovery process itself.

For example, in a controlled open-source evaluation, original source code may be used after recovery to compare the recovered result with the known original.

A validation reference is not recovery input.

---

# 6. Observation

An **Observation** is information identified directly from Evidence without relying on a recovery inference about what the original system probably contained or intended.

Examples include discovering a type in an assembly or reading a configuration value.

An Observation is direct extraction from Evidence. A Recovered Fact is a normalized proposition directly entailed by admissible Observations without inferential leap, assumptions, or Developer Assertions.

---

# 7. Evidence

**Evidence** is information that can support a recovery conclusion.

LegacyRevive.NET must distinguish evidence originating from the original system from information introduced later by developer intervention or reconstruction.

Developer Input sits beside Evidence as a distinct top-level Recovery Input category. Developer Assertions may influence inference but are not formal Evidence.

---

# 8. Evidence Source

An **Evidence Source** is the origin from which an evidence-bearing observation or item was obtained.

Examples may include:

- an original assembly;
- a configuration file;
- a PDB;
- package metadata;
- explicitly admitted independent contextual evidence;
- another explicitly identified evidence source.

This is a glossary concept, not yet a commitment to a particular implementation entity.

---

# 9. Evidence Item

An **Evidence Item** is a discrete piece of evidence represented by the recovery system so that its origin and relevance can be tracked.

This is a conceptual term.

Its exact data-model representation belongs in `03-evidence-model.md`.

---

# 10. Recovered Fact

A **Recovered Fact** is a proposition treated by the recovery process as directly supported by sufficiently strong evidence rather than merely inferred.

A recovered fact is not necessarily information that existed textually in one artifact; it may be a normalized proposition supported by direct evidence.

A Recovered Fact requires direct entailment from admissible Observations through deterministic, semantics-preserving interpretation. If a proposition requires heuristic inference, assumptions, Developer Assertions, or reconstructed-output support, it is not a Recovered Fact.

An inference must not silently become a recovered fact merely because it is useful. If later independent Evidence directly establishes it, a new Recovered Fact supersedes the earlier Inference while preserving history.

---

# 11. Derived Observation

A **Derived Observation** is a pattern, relationship, aggregation, or other observation produced from one or more underlying observations without claiming unsupported historical intent.

Examples may include a recurring namespace pattern or repeated dependency convention.

Derived observations remain traceable to their supporting material.

They are not automatically facts about original developer intent.

---

# 12. Inference

An **Inference** is a recovery conclusion that goes beyond what can be established directly and is derived from evidence, observations, assumptions, heuristics, or other permitted recovery inputs.

An inference must remain distinguishable from a directly supported fact.

The basis for a material inference should be explainable.

---

# 13. Strongly Supported Inference

A **Strongly Supported Inference** is an inference for which the available supporting evidence is substantial and mutually consistent, but which is still not treated as directly established fact.

`Strongly Supported` is one of the canonical inferential support-strength labels established by `EVID-006` / D027. It remains an Inference label and never turns the conclusion into a Recovered Fact.

---

# 14. Heuristic Inference

A **Heuristic Inference** is an inference produced using a recovery heuristic rather than direct evidence alone.

It should retain the evidence and heuristic basis that contributed to it.

A heuristic inference must not be presented as fact merely because the heuristic often performs well.

---

# 15. Assumption

An **Assumption** is a proposition temporarily accepted so recovery work can progress despite insufficient evidence to establish it as fact.

Assumptions must remain visible and must not silently become facts.

Canonical Assumption states are `Active`, `Superseded`, `Invalidated`, and `Retired`. Invalidating an assumption requires materially dependent downstream conclusions to be reevaluated.

---

# 16. Uncertainty

**Uncertainty** is the condition in which available recovery information does not justify a single sufficiently supported conclusion.

Uncertainty may arise from:

- missing evidence;
- weak evidence;
- ambiguous evidence;
- conflicting evidence;
- reliance on assumptions;
- unresolved developer decisions.

Uncertainty is a legitimate recovery state.

---

# 17. Unresolved

**Unresolved** describes a recovery question or conclusion for which the available information is insufficient to adopt a final recovery conclusion.

Unresolved is not synonymous with failure.

It indicates that the uncertainty remains explicit.

---

# 18. Conflicting Evidence

**Conflicting Evidence** is evidence that supports incompatible or materially inconsistent recovery conclusions.

Conflicting evidence must remain visible rather than being silently discarded.

A Conflict explicitly links incompatible propositions/conclusions and their support paths. Resolution preserves the competing evidence and historical conclusions rather than deleting them.

---

# 19. Provenance

**Provenance** describes where recovery information came from.

It should allow important recovery conclusions to be traced back to their origin.

Provenance is a foundational requirement of LegacyRevive.NET.

Provenance is the umbrella traceability concept and includes both origin provenance and derivation lineage.

---

# 20. Lineage

**Lineage** describes the derivation history through which recovery information was produced from earlier information.

For example, it may describe how observations contributed to an inference and how that inference contributed to a reconstruction.

Lineage remains useful vocabulary for derivation history, but it is formally represented as part of Provenance rather than as a separate top-level traceability concept.

---

# 21. Confidence

**Confidence** describes the degree of support for a recovery conclusion based on its evidence and provenance.

Confidence should be explainable and should not imply arbitrary statistical precision.

The canonical inferential support-strength labels are:

- **Strongly Supported**;
- **Plausible**;
- **Tentative**.

`Unresolved` is a recovery state rather than a confidence label.

Support strength attaches to Inferences and inferential reconstruction decisions. It does not attach directly to raw Evidence Sources, Observations, Recovered Facts, Assumptions, whole reconstructed artifacts as one undifferentiated score, or Validation Results.

---

# 22. Reconstruction

**Reconstruction** is the process of creating a development representation that did not survive directly but is produced from available recovery information.

A reconstruction may include inferred or generated:

- project structure;
- solution structure;
- source;
- configuration;
- dependency declarations;
- other development artifacts.

Reconstruction does not mean exact reproduction.

---

# 23. Candidate Reconstruction

A **Candidate Reconstruction** is a proposed reconstruction that has not yet been accepted as the current recovery result.

It may be evaluated, compared, modified, rejected, or replaced.

This is a conceptual term; its implementation representation is not yet fixed.

---

# 24. Reconstructed Artifact

A **Reconstructed Artifact** is an artifact produced by the recovery process rather than an original surviving artifact.

A reconstructed artifact must never become independent original evidence merely because it exists.

Its origin must remain distinguishable from original-system material.

---

# 25. Recovered Source

**Recovered Source** is source code produced or restored through the recovery process.

It may include decompiled, reconstructed, generated, repaired, or developer-adjusted source.

Recovered source is not necessarily the original source.

---

# 26. Original Source

**Original Source** is the source code that belonged to the original development codebase.

Where original source is unavailable during recovery but available for controlled evaluation, it must remain a Validation Reference rather than Recovery Input.

---

# 27. Recovered Structure

**Recovered Structure** is reconstructed or recovered structural information about the system, such as:

- projects;
- project relationships;
- namespaces;
- type relationships;
- solution organization.

Recovered structure is not necessarily identical to the original structure.

---

# 28. Project Boundary

A **Project Boundary** is the boundary that determines which recovered items are grouped into a particular reconstructed .NET project.

A recovered project boundary may be directly supported, inferred, developer-selected, or otherwise reconstructed.

Its exact recovery rules belong in `08-project-reconstruction.md`.

---

# 29. Developer Intervention

**Developer Intervention** is deliberate human participation in the recovery process intended to help recovery progress.

It may include supplying information, resolving ambiguity, correcting a result, choosing between alternatives, or making a practical recovery decision.

Developer intervention is first-class but must remain distinguishable from original-system evidence.

---

# 30. Developer Assertion

A **Developer Assertion** is a proposition supplied by a developer for use by the recovery process.

It may represent knowledge that is unavailable from surviving artifacts.

A developer assertion is not automatically original-system evidence.

A Developer Assertion is Developer Input, not formal Evidence. It may influence inference but cannot by itself make a proposition a Recovered Fact.

---

# 31. Developer Decision

A **Developer Decision** is a deliberate choice made by a developer about how the recovery should proceed when the available information does not uniquely determine the answer.

A developer decision must remain distinguishable from a claim about what historically existed.

---

# 32. Developer Correction

A **Developer Correction** is a developer intervention that replaces or corrects an earlier recovery conclusion or reconstruction.

A correction should preserve what it changed rather than erasing the earlier reasoning history.

A Developer Correction creates or supports a new conclusion/reconstruction decision that supersedes the earlier item while retaining history. Materially dependent downstream items become `Needs Reevaluation` until recomputed or reaffirmed and become `Invalidated` where valid support no longer remains.

---

# 33. Convention Observation

A **Convention Observation** is a derived observation describing a recurring pattern in surviving recovery information that may help guide later interventions or reconstruction.

It may concern naming, namespaces, dependencies, configuration, project organization, or coding patterns.

A convention observation is not proof of Original Developer Intent.

---

# 34. Original Developer Intent

**Original Developer Intent** is the historical motivation, purpose, or reasoning of the original developers.

LegacyRevive.NET should not claim original developer intent unless it is supported by suitable surviving evidence.

Observed conventions or reconstructed choices do not by themselves establish intent.

---

# 35. Recovery Process

The **Recovery Process** is the overall sequence of activities through which LegacyRevive.NET moves from available recovery input toward a recovered development baseline.

This glossary does not define the exact stages.

The authoritative process model belongs in `07-recovery-process.md`.

---

# 36. Recovery Result

A **Recovery Result** is an output or conclusion produced by the recovery process.

It may include facts, observations, inferences, reconstructions, diagnostics, validation results, or other recovery outputs.

This is a conceptual umbrella term rather than a fixed implementation type.

---

# 37. Recovery Baseline

A **Recovery Baseline** is a coherent recovery state that provides a reference point for further development, validation, repair, or subsequent recovery work.

It need not represent complete recovery.

---

# 38. Workable Recovered Solution

A **Workable Recovered Solution** is a recovered .NET development representation that provides enough useful and trustworthy material for a developer to continue understanding, building where possible, validating, repairing, and maintaining the application.

It does not imply exact historical reproduction or behavioural equivalence.

---

# 39. Validation

**Validation** is the process of evaluating a recovery result against one or more criteria or references.

Validation may assess different aspects of fidelity independently.

The detailed methodology belongs in `10-validation-strategy.md`.

---

# 40. Validation Result

**Validation Result** is retained only as generic umbrella wording for outcome material produced by validation.

The canonical validation records are more specific:

- a **Validation Finding** records the outcome of one Validation Check;
- a **Validation Run** records the governed execution/history for a Validation Plan and Target;
- **Recovery Metric** results summarize declared populations and measurements without replacing Validation Findings.

New design and implementation should prefer those canonical terms rather than introducing a separate undifferentiated Validation Result entity. Detailed validation semantics belong to `10-validation-strategy.md`; metric-result semantics belong to `11-recovery-metrics.md`.

---

# 41. Structural Similarity

**Structural Similarity** describes the degree to which recovered structure resembles a known reference structure.

Similarity does not imply identity or behavioural equivalence.

---

# 42. Behavioural Equivalence

**Behavioural Equivalence** is the condition in which a recovered system behaves equivalently to the relevant original system for the behaviour being evaluated.

Compilation alone does not establish behavioural equivalence.

---

# 43. Buildability

**Buildability** is the property that a recovered solution or project can be successfully built under the relevant build conditions.

Buildability is an important recovery milestone but does not prove behavioural equivalence.

---

# 44. Similarity

**Similarity** means that two items share relevant characteristics according to a stated comparison basis.

Similarity does not mean equivalence.

---

# 45. Equivalence

**Equivalence** means that two items can be treated as the same for a specific defined purpose or property.

Equivalence must always be understood relative to the property being evaluated.

---

# 46. Recovery Heuristic

A **Recovery Heuristic** is a rule, pattern, strategy, or approximation used to help infer or reconstruct information when direct evidence is insufficient.

A heuristic should be explainable, testable where practical, and should not convert its output into fact without sufficient support.

---

# 47. Heuristic Research

**Heuristic Research** is the study of known codebases or recovery cases to evaluate whether proposed recovery heuristics are useful beyond a single target.

Research-derived patterns remain heuristics when later applied to a particular recovery target.

Research material is not direct evidence about that target.

---

# 48. Recovery Target

The **Recovery Target** is the legacy .NET system, application, solution, project set, or deployed artifact set that LegacyRevive.NET is attempting to recover.

---

# 49. Source Evidence

**Source Evidence** is evidence relevant to the recovery of source-level information.

The term does not mean original source code necessarily exists.

It may include information from metadata, PDBs, documentation, decompilation inputs, or other original artifacts that support source recovery.

This is a descriptive term, not a separate top-level evidence category unless the Evidence Model later defines it as one.

---

# 50. Intervention Record

An **Intervention Record** is the committed, provenance-bearing representation of a Developer Intervention.

It records the intervention kind, content/choice, scope, status, attribution/provenance, and affected recovery target/question where applicable.

Committed Intervention Records may be Active, Superseded, or Withdrawn.

---

# 51. Developer Intervention Conflict

A **Developer Intervention Conflict** exists when two or more Active interventions provide incompatible directions or propositions over overlapping scope.

The conflict must be resolved explicitly rather than by incidental execution order.

---

# 52. Withdrawn Intervention

A **Withdrawn Intervention** is an Intervention Record that remains historically preserved but no longer influences current recovery.

Withdrawal does not imply the intervention was historically false.

Materially dependent downstream items require reevaluation.

---

# 53. Reaffirmation

**Reaffirmation** is a traceable decision that a recovery item marked `Needs Reevaluation` remains valid after an upstream intervention changes.

Reaffirmation must record why the item remains justified.

It must not merely clear the reevaluation state without explanation.

---

# 54. Needs Reevaluation

**Needs Reevaluation** is a current-state marker indicating that a material dependency changed and the affected recovery item must be recomputed or deliberately reaffirmed before it can be treated as current.

---

# 55. Convention Support Profile

A **Convention Support Profile** is the descriptive support information attached to a Convention Observation.

It may include supporting instance counts, independent source counts, observed scope, coverage where meaningful, known exceptions, inferential dependencies, and whether Developer Input influenced discovery or scoping.

It is not an inferential confidence label.

---

# 56. Convention Conflict

A **Convention Conflict** exists when materially incompatible Convention Observations claim overlapping scope such that both cannot guide the same unresolved recovery decision without further choice.

Both observations, scopes, support profiles, provenance, and exceptions remain visible.

---

# 57. Convention Generalization

**Convention Generalization** is the creation of a broader-scope convention conclusion from narrower Convention Observations.

Generalization requires its own support and provenance.

It must not silently widen the scope of an existing Convention Observation.

---

# 58. Recovery Contribution

A **Recovery Contribution** describes what a surviving artifact can provide to the recovery process.

Recovery Matrix contributions are classified as:

- **Direct**;
- **Inferable**;
- **Heuristic**;
- **Not established by this artifact alone**.

The classification describes the relationship between the artifact and the proposed recovery information.

---

# 59. Artifact Capability

**Artifact Capability** describes the kinds of recovery information an artifact type may contain or support.

Artifact Capability is theoretical/type-level.

It does not mean that a particular artifact instance actually contains that information.

---

# 60. Artifact-Instance Profile

An **Artifact-Instance Profile** records what LegacyRevive.NET actually detected and recovered from a specific supplied artifact.

It may include:

- detected format/type;
- direct Observations;
- candidate inferential contributions;
- limitations;
- companion-artifact relationships;
- unsupported/corrupt/unresolved conditions.

---

# 61. Recovery Ceiling

A **Recovery Ceiling** describes information that is not established or not reliably recoverable from the currently available artifact set.

Unless justified by stronger evidence, a recovery ceiling is scoped to the available recovery context rather than claiming universal impossibility.

---

# 62. Recovery Workspace

A **Recovery Workspace** is the durable recovery context containing recovery inputs, evidence-model state, intervention history, reconstruction state, diagnostics, and related recovery artifacts.

A workspace may contain multiple Recovery Runs.

---

# 63. Recovery Run

A **Recovery Run** is one execution through some or all applicable recovery stages using a particular workspace state, tool version, configuration, and active intervention set.

---

# 64. Recovery Checkpoint

A **Recovery Checkpoint** is an identified reproducible state of a Recovery Workspace at a meaningful point in recovery.

It supports replay, comparison, debugging, and reevaluation.

---

# 65. Recovery Plan

A **Recovery Plan** is derived recovery state describing what reconstruction work may proceed under the current evidence, inference, assumptions, conflicts, and committed interventions.

It is not Original-System Evidence.

---

# 66. Partial Recovery

**Partial Recovery** is a useful recovery state in which some stages or dimensions have produced valid results while other work remains incomplete.

Partial does not mean failed.

---

# 67. Blocked Recovery

**Blocked Recovery** is a process state in which progress for a defined recovery question or stage requires unavailable information, intervention, or capability.

Blocked does not establish that the original system is unrecoverable.

---

# 68. Validated Baseline

A **Validated Baseline** is a Workable Recovery Baseline that has undergone the applicable validation process and records its validation status/results.

It does not imply perfect historical identity unless a defined validation result establishes equivalence for a particular dimension.

---

# 69. Candidate Project

A **Candidate Project** is a reconstructed .NET project boundary proposed for the current recovery state.

It is a Candidate Reconstruction and is not automatically an Original Project.

---

# 70. Original Project

An **Original Project** is a project that actually existed in the original development codebase.

An Original Project must not be assumed merely because LegacyRevive.NET generated a Candidate Project.

---

# 71. Candidate Solution

A **Candidate Solution** is a reconstructed organizational container for Candidate Projects.

It does not prove that an identical original `.sln` existed.

---

# 72. Project Boundary Support Profile

A **Project Boundary Support Profile** describes the recovery information supporting a Candidate Project boundary, including direct evidence, inferential support, conventions, Developer Decisions, and known contradictory signals.

---

# 73. Project Boundary Conflict

A **Project Boundary Conflict** exists when materially supported recovery signals favor incompatible Candidate Project groupings.

The competing support must remain visible until the boundary is resolved or an operational candidate is selected.

---

# 74. Project Kind

**Project Kind** is a reconstructed property describing the Candidate Project's development role, such as library or executable/host, based on the strongest available recovery state.

It is inferential unless direct surviving project metadata establishes it.

---

# 75. Unresolved Project Dependency

An **Unresolved Project Dependency** is a dependency observed or required by a Candidate Project that cannot yet be resolved to another Candidate Project, package, framework component, or available assembly representation.

---

# 76. Workable Candidate Project

A **Workable Candidate Project** is a Candidate Project coherent enough to support continued engineering work while retaining explicit unresolved historical or dependency questions.

Workable does not mean historically exact.

---

# 77. Preserved Source Content

**Preserved Source Content** is source or source-derived text surviving directly in admissible Original Artifacts.

---

# 78. Metadata-Reconstructed Source

**Metadata-Reconstructed Source** is generated source syntax representing directly recoverable program metadata/signatures.

---

# 79. Decompiler-Generated Source

**Decompiler-Generated Source** is source-language code synthesized from compiled implementation information and is not automatically Original Source.

---

# 80. Inferred Source Structure

**Inferred Source Structure** is reconstructed source-file/folder/layout organization not directly preserved as complete Original Source structure.

---

# 81. Developer-Adjusted Source

**Developer-Adjusted Source** is reconstructed source changed through Developer Intervention.

---

# 82. Recovered Source File

A **Recovered Source File** is a stable source-reconstruction entity whose current path/name and content may combine multiple provenance classes.

---

# 83. Source Skeleton

A **Source Skeleton** is a partial reconstructed source representation containing declarations/signatures without claiming complete implementation bodies.

---

# 84. Source Candidate

A **Source Candidate** is one materially plausible reconstructed source representation among alternatives.

---

# 85. Workable Recovered Source

**Workable Recovered Source** is source reconstruction coherent enough for continued engineering use while retaining provenance and unresolved limitations.

---

# 86. Validation Target

A **Validation Target** is the identified recovery result/checkpoint being evaluated by a Validation Run.

Material changes to the recovery state create a new Validation Target.

---

# 87. Validation Reference Snapshot

A **Validation Reference Snapshot** is the specific identified state of a Validation Reference used for comparison.

It may correspond to a repository commit/archive, original build state, expected behavior set, or another controlled reference state.

---

# 88. Validation Plan

A **Validation Plan** defines the dimensions, checks, prerequisites, target/reference context, environment, and intentional non-evaluation for a Validation Run.

---

# 89. Validation Dimension

A **Validation Dimension** is a distinct aspect of recovery quality, such as structure, dependencies, API, source, buildability, or behavior.

Dimensions remain independently visible.

---

# 90. Validation Check

A **Validation Check** is one defined comparison/test within a Validation Dimension.

---

# 91. Validation Finding

A **Validation Finding** records the result and diagnostic basis of a Validation Check.

It must distinguish divergence from inability to evaluate/compare and from validation execution error.

---

# 92. Validation Run

A **Validation Run** is one execution of a Validation Plan against an identified Validation Target and, where applicable, a Validation Reference Snapshot.

---

# 93. Blind Validation Run

A **Blind Validation Run** is a controlled evaluation in which the Validation Reference is unavailable to recovery until the Validation Target has been frozen.

---

# 94. Not Evaluated

**Not Evaluated** means a potentially applicable validation dimension/check was intentionally or practically not executed in that Validation Run.

It is neither success nor failure.

---

# 95. Not Comparable

**Not Comparable** means the available Validation Target, Reference, or context does not support a defensible comparison for that dimension/check.

It is neither success nor failure.

---

# 96. Fundamental non-equivalences

LegacyRevive.NET must preserve these distinctions:

```text
Evidence ≠ inference
Inference ≠ fact
Recovered source ≠ original source
Recovered structure ≠ necessarily original structure
Compilable ≠ behaviourally equivalent
Similarity ≠ equivalence
Convention observation ≠ original developer intent
Developer intervention ≠ original evidence
Validation reference ≠ recovery input
Reconstructed artifact ≠ original artifact
```

These distinctions are foundational.

The detailed formal semantics behind several of them remain delegated to the Evidence Model.

# 100. Recovery Metric

A **Recovery Metric** is a named measurement computed over an explicitly defined recovery/validation population, with declared applicability, comparison/matching rule, numerator, denominator where applicable, and provenance.

Detailed metric semantics are owned by `11-recovery-metrics.md`.

---

# 101. Metric Population

A **Metric Population** is the explicitly bounded set of items eligible to contribute to a Recovery Metric.

Population accounting distinguishes comparable/evaluated items from Not Evaluated, Not Comparable, Not Applicable, unknown/abstained, and execution-error states where relevant.

---

# 102. Recovery Coverage

**Recovery Coverage** measures how much of an eligible/comparable recovery population LegacyRevive.NET established or attempted, independently of whether the established conclusions were correct.

Coverage is distinct from correctness.

---

# 103. Recoverability-Aware Effectiveness

**Recoverability-Aware Effectiveness** measures recovery performance against items that the allowed Recovery Input could reasonably establish under the canonical recoverability model.

It remains distinct from raw historical-reference fidelity; historically unavailable information must not be silently erased from reporting.

---

# 104. Epistemic Accuracy

**Epistemic Accuracy** is the quality of LegacyRevive.NET's handling of inferential correctness, uncertainty, abstention/Unknown states, conflict detection, support-strength calibration, provenance validity, and circular-support protection.

It is distinct from source/structural fidelity and from operational buildability.

---

# 105. Recovery Orchestrator

The **Recovery Orchestrator** is the application-level coordinator that executes/resumes the canonical Recovery Process by invoking analysis, integration, inference, intervention, reconstruction, checkpoint, build-feedback, and validation capabilities without owning their domain-specific algorithms.

---

# 106. Artifact Analyzer

An **Artifact Analyzer** is a capability that inspects admissible recovery artifacts and emits provenance-bearing direct Observations and diagnostics.

It does not silently promote inferential architectural conclusions into direct observations.

---

# 107. Recovery Knowledge Store

The **Recovery Knowledge Store** is the logical state area containing recovery conclusions/history and their provenance/support relationships, including Observations, Recovered Facts, Derived Observations, Inferences, Assumptions, Conflicts, and supersession/current-state links.

The term is architectural and does not prescribe a physical database.

---

# 108. Reconstruction Workspace

A **Reconstruction Workspace** is the logical state area containing Candidate Projects, Source Candidates, generated project/source/configuration artifacts, build-repair state, and reconstruction selections.

Its generated contents remain Reconstructed Artifacts rather than Original-System Evidence.

---

# 109. Intervention Journal

The **Intervention Journal** is the logical append/history-preserving state area for committed Developer Assertions, Developer Decisions, Developer Corrections, their scope/status/provenance, and supersession/withdrawal relationships.

---

# 110. Support Graph

The **Support Graph** is the directed provenance/dependency representation linking material recovery conclusions to the evidence, facts, derived observations, assumptions, interventions, rules, and upstream conclusions on which they depend.

It is used to enforce traceability, downstream reevaluation, and circular/self-support protection.

---

# 111. Capability Port

A **Capability Port** is an inward-owned contract through which the recovery application/domain accesses an external or replaceable capability such as metadata inspection, decompilation, build execution, persistence, package inspection, or process execution.

Concrete implementations are adapters selected by the canonical tooling and implementation design.

---

# 112. Architecture Module

An **Architecture Module** is a logical responsibility/dependency boundary in LegacyRevive.NET.

A module does not by itself imply a separate process, service, NuGet package, or assembly.

---

# 113. Tool Adapter

A **Tool Adapter** is a concrete implementation of a Capability Port that invokes or wraps an external/platform tool or library while translating its outputs into LegacyRevive.NET canonical domain/application contracts.

Tool output does not define epistemic category by itself.

---

# 114. External Enrichment

**External Enrichment** is recovery-supporting information obtained from a source that was not part of the Supplied Recovery Input, such as a remote NuGet feed.

When explicitly enabled and admitted to a recovery, External Enrichment is represented as Independent Contextual Evidence within Recovery Input. It remains provenance-distinct from Original-System Evidence and from Supplied Recovery Input and must not be reported as if directly recovered from supplied artifacts. Validation Reference remains outside Recovery Input and cannot be converted into enrichment for the same blind recovery lineage.

---

# 115. Tool Provenance

**Tool Provenance** identifies the tool/library, effective version, settings, environment, and inputs materially responsible for a generated observation, reconstruction, build result, validation result, or metric result.

It supports reproducibility but does not by itself make the tool output historically authoritative.

---

# 116. Dependency Baseline

A **Dependency Baseline** is the centrally pinned set of implementation package/tool versions selected for a LegacyRevive.NET release or benchmark context.

Changing a baseline is distinct from changing the subject application's recovered dependency model.

---

# 117. MVP Recovery

An **MVP Recovery** is a recovery executed within the capability boundary defined by `14-mvp.md`, beginning from a local artifact directory and producing a persisted, inspectable candidate development baseline or an explicit Partial/Blocked result.

It does not imply a successful build.

---

# 118. MVP Release Corpus

The **MVP Release Corpus** is the controlled set of known-reference subjects and artifact scenarios used to gate the first LegacyRevive.NET release through blind validation and the canonical recovery metrics.

Subjects used to tune inference rules should not constitute the entire held-out release corpus.

---

# 119. MVP Release Gate

An **MVP Release Gate** is a mandatory acceptance condition covering end-to-end function, epistemic safety, deterministic replay, persistence/history, recovery quality, or developer usability.

Release gates are not collapsed into one global recovery score.

---

# 120. Candidate Development Baseline

A **Candidate Development Baseline** is the exported development representation produced by recovery, including Candidate Projects, reconstructed source, dependencies/configuration/resources where supported, provenance/reporting, and known unresolved issues.

It may be Partial, Blocked, or Workable and is not represented as the exact original repository.

---

# 121. Roadmap Horizon

A **Roadmap Horizon** is an evidence- and dependency-gated post-MVP capability grouping.

It expresses strategic sequencing rather than a fixed release date or accepted detailed implementation design.

---

# 122. Capability Promotion

**Capability Promotion** is the governed transition of a roadmap candidate into active design/implementation scope after product/research evidence justifies the work.

Promotion does not bypass qualification, decision, audit, or canonical ownership rules.

---

# 123. Roadmap Candidate

A **Roadmap Candidate** is a capability considered worthwhile for future investigation whose detailed scope, ordering, or feasibility remains contingent on evidence.

It is not a promised feature.

---

# 124. Planned Horizon

A **Planned Horizon** identifies the strategic location of a capability after the MVP without making its implementation details canonical.

---

# 125. Original-System Evidence

**Original-System Evidence** is Evidence that survived from the target system itself, including admissible original artifacts and information directly preserved with that system.

It must remain distinguishable from Independent Contextual Evidence, Developer Input, Reconstruction, and Validation Reference.

---

# 126. Independent Contextual Evidence

**Independent Contextual Evidence** is non-generated information obtained independently of the supplied target artifacts and explicitly admitted to help interpret the recovery target.

It is formal Evidence when admitted, but it is not Original-System Evidence and must retain provenance showing its external origin. External Enrichment is represented in this category when enabled for recovery.

---

# 127. Developer Input

**Developer Input** is the top-level Recovery Input category containing Developer Assertions, Developer Decisions, and Developer Corrections.

Developer Input may influence recovery conclusions and reconstruction but is not formal Evidence and cannot by itself create a Recovered Fact.

---

# 128. Recovery Configuration

**Recovery Configuration** is the Recovery Input category that controls how a recovery run is performed, such as enabled capabilities or recovery options.

Recovery Configuration may influence processing but is not Evidence about what historically existed unless separately supported by admissible Evidence.

---

# 129. Conflict

A **Conflict** is an explicit recovery record linking materially incompatible propositions or conclusions together with their support/provenance paths and current resolution state.

Resolving a Conflict does not erase the competing historical evidence or conclusions, and an operational Developer Decision does not retroactively prove historical correctness.

---

# 130. Unknown

**Unknown** is an explicit abstention or classification state used when the available admissible information does not justify a supported value or conclusion for the relevant question.

Unknown is legitimate recovery state. It is not automatically correct or incorrect, and it is distinct from `Not Evaluated`, `Not Comparable`, and from an unresolved design qualification. Domain-specific models may use `Unresolved` when the emphasis is an open recovery question rather than an unassigned value.

---

# 131. Supplied Recovery Input

**Supplied Recovery Input** is the portion of Recovery Input deliberately provided to the recovery before or directly as part of the recovery scenario, such as local artifacts, supplied configuration, and developer-provided inputs.

It excludes material fetched later through External Enrichment. Externally retrieved material may become Independent Contextual Evidence when explicitly admitted, but it does not thereby become Supplied Recovery Input.

---

# 132. Workable Recovery Baseline

A **Workable Recovery Baseline** is a coherent Recovery Baseline that is suitable for continued engineering work under the criteria owned by `07-recovery-process.md`.

It may retain explicit Unknowns, unresolved items, incomplete source recovery, or limited validation. It does not imply exact historical reproduction or behavioural equivalence.


---

# 133. Materialized Reconstruction

A **Materialized Reconstruction** is the developer-facing filesystem realization of the current active reconstructed development state, such as generated solution/project/source/configuration files exposed in a Recovery Workspace for use by ordinary IDEs, editors, and build tools.

It remains a Reconstructed Artifact representation. Incidental filesystem mutation of the materialization does not by itself change canonical recovery state.

---

# 134. Working-Tree Divergence

**Working-Tree Divergence** is a material difference between the reconstructed files currently materialized in the developer-facing workspace and the canonical recovery state from which that materialization is expected to derive.

Working-Tree Divergence is operational workspace state. It is not by itself Evidence, Developer Input, a committed Developer Intervention, or Developer-Adjusted Source. A divergent change becomes Developer-Adjusted Source only through explicit governed adoption; it may instead be explicitly discarded and rematerialized from canonical state.
